## Digest

- **Measured night spreads are several times the scalp target.** In 2009 Interactive Brokers quoted EURCHF at 4 to 7.5 pips and EURGBP at 2.5 to 3.5 pips in the Asian session (EN8-22). In 2008 Alpari's overnight EURGBP spread rose to 5 pips (EN8-40). Users measured rollover spikes of 5-10 pips on GBPUSD and about 20 pips on GBPNZD (EN8-16), and over 20 pips on CHF and NZD crosses (EN8-33, excerpt only). One broker's support said night spreads "are not limited" and can reach about 100 points (EN8-10).
- **The rollover window lasts longer than one minute.** Coders saw the 22:00 GMT widening last 1 to 5 minutes, with a second widening about 1 hour later on some symbols (EN8-04). Forum users report spreads at several times normal for about 1 hour after the NY close, and a 1-hour shift during DST mismatch weeks (EN8-16). A StrategyQuant user blocked the swap minute to avoid about 40-pip spreads (EN8-34).
- **Costs, not signals, kill Asian reversion on EURGBP.** The same synthetic-cross reversion lost money in Asia (220 trades, net negative) and made money in Europe. In that data the Asian spread was about 90x the European one (EN8-12). A gold study removed Asia as "high-spread" (EN8-28). EURGBP has the smallest Asian range relative to London (London/Asia 1.79x) (EN8-26).
- **Limit orders at rollover get bad fills that backtests cannot show.** On a real IC Markets USDCHF trade, 2 of about 10 identical accounts got about 221 points of negative slippage (EN8-02). The NoCap FX vendor says buyers' results differ from his signal because "rollover spreads are too wide" (EN8-42). Bar-close signals on AUDNZD filled about 29 pips away on the next bar (EN8-43).
- **Broker dependence is large and measurable.** M15 OHLC bars differed by 12% of the candle range on average between two brokers, and quiet-hour bars differed most (EN8-38). Average all-in cost on 28 pairs was about 1.5 pips at IC Markets RAW and 1.2 at Dukascopy, against the 0.5 pips a portfolio EA needed (EN8-24).
- **Crowding and broker reaction are an old decay mechanism.** In Dec 2008, users linked wider overnight EURGBP spreads to FAPTurbo (about 160k copies sold) and said brokers closed accounts running it (EN8-40). A 2009 thread asks why most EURGBP scalpers stopped working after 2007 (EN8-11).
- **The long verified night record is thin.** Night Hunter Pro 9 Pairs: about 6 years live on IC Markets, +191%, PF 1.20, max DD 22% by balance, negative current month and 0 subscribers (EN8-05). The NCM Asian scalper (2018-2020, +167%, DD 15%) survived only by averaging into losers (EN8-18).
- **Post-2022 out-of-sample tests are mixed.** An open-source night scalper with stated costs (night spread 0.45-2.0 pips, $7 per lot) stayed positive on 2022-2026 data, even at 1.5x spread. AUDNZD was the best pair (PF 4.12). The author warns of selection bias from about 2,400 combinations and expects live results to be worse (EN8-15). A gold Asia fade failed its 2024-2026 holdout (PF 0.90) (EN8-09).
- **Live, demo and tester results diverge at night.** Examples: AUDCAD reversion took no trades in 2 live months although the backtest traded (EN8-07). A night trade that won in the tester stayed open live (EN8-10). IBFX live results were worse than demo (EN8-40).
- **Practical controls that individuals use.** Block the 22:00 GMT hour and filter on the live spread against a running average (EN8-04). Hard-code session hours for each broker's server time (EN8-34). Log p95 spreads with free tools (EN8-30). Some lift stop-losses during rollover (EN8-17, EN8-31), which adds its own risk.
- **Tail risk on CHF crosses.** On 15 Jan 2015, EURCHF moved about -3,300 pips and CADCHF about -1,500 pips in one day (EN8-14). Range grids on AUDNZD carry the standard objection that a trend wipes them out (EN8-25).
- **London fix: the effect is real, narrow and shrinking.** Extreme moves happen in the minute around 16:00 and weakened for some pairs after June 2013 (EN8-X3). Some price anomaly survived the 2015 reform (EN8-X4). Month-end fix impact changes a lot by month and pair (AUD, CAD and NZD largest). Q2 2023 was the smallest since 2021, with no stable post-fix reversal (EN8-X2, EN8-X5). Practitioners say the volume rush before the fix is "not so much anymore" (EN8-06).
- **No live record by an individual was found for fix, month-end or Tokyo-fix trading in the English sources I could read.** Retail fix-fade posts give no data (EN8-27). The only fix "strategy" numbers are an academic illustration (Sharpe 3.43 out of sample, "for illustration only") (EN8-X1). Calendar data show a quarter-end volatility bump and that EURCHF is the least volatile G10 pair (EN8-29).
