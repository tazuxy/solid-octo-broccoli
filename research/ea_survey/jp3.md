## Digest

Based on 38 entries, all excerpt-based. The search budget ran out after 18 queries, so this falls well short of the 100 entries planned.

1. **Long verified live records (≥12 months):** none in this slice. The longest forward record is LB-EA, about 11 months on a public but demo MT5 account (jp3-014). Its result comes from money management (betting-system lot progression, from the martingale family), not from entry logic. The only multi-month non-demo record is an AI trend system: 7 months, 534 trades, +11.5% (jp3-030). It has no third-party verification.
2. **Common settings:** BB/RSI reversal EAs in the retail templates use the same building blocks we tested: RSI OB/OS, a BB touch or close outside the band, and an early-JST time window with fixed TP/SL (jp3-011, 013). Divergence EAs run on H1 with 1% risk (jp3-004). One early-morning scalper explicitly avoids grid and uses a pair-correlation filter instead (jp3-010).
3. **Typical fates:**
   - Decay from stale optimisation (jp3-023).
   - Forward drawdown going past the backtest maximum, which led the owner to stop the EA (jp3-024).
   - Regime change: an AI trend model failed in the mid-2025 range market, with live win rate at 57.5% against 85.7% in simulation (jp3-030).
   - A one-day blow-up of 500k JPY (jp3-025).
   - A live record ended because of account-freeze risk with the broker or copy-trade platform (jp3-018).
4. **Possibly missed by our tests:**
   - A forward-DD-vs-backtest-DD kill switch (jp3-024).
   - Pair-correlation and volatility-regime filters (jp3-010, 032).
   - Broker execution and account risk (jp3-018, 020, 035).
   
   Independent individuals report the same broad null result we found. An RSI reversal EA ran at PF 1.05 and lost money in demo (jp3-013). In a 5,000-pattern search almost everything failed, and shorter timeframes were worse (jp3-022). A bot with a 66% win rate lost money once spread was included (jp3-021).
