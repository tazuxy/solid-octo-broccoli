## Digest

**Recurring ideas.** Flows around FX fixes, drift and reversal around FOMC, NFP and CPI, cross-sectional carry/value/momentum and carry crash risk, weekend gaps, gold intraday and seasonal patterns, and session-range breakouts.

**Most promising new rules to test:**
1. **Daily fix reversal (M-EN-01, 07).** Long USD into the 9:55 Tokyo, 14:15 ECB and 16:00 London fixes, then short USD for 1-2 h after. Reason: dealers pre-position and unwind every day, not just at month-end.
2. **FOMC drift and reversal (03-04).** Hold the foreign currency from T-2 to the announcement, then fade. Post-FOMC returns cancel about 65% of the pre-drift.
3. **Round numbers (22).** Fade the first touch of xx.00, and treat a break of 10-15 pips beyond as continuation. Reason: take-profit orders cluster at the round number, stop-losses just past it.
4. **Weekend-gap fade (37).** Fade only gaps above k x ATR, entering after the first hour.
5. **Gold (47-52).** Long XAUUSD in Asian hours, flat in London/NY. Seasonal long in September and November only.
6. **Intraday momentum (30).** The first 30 min of the session predicts the last 30 min, on indices and FX.
7. **News second wave (64-68).** Enter 10-30 min after the release instead of straddling it.

**Warnings practitioners report:**
- The London breakout decayed and lost money from 2016 to 2026.
- Results depend on broker server time and DST.
- At news releases, spread widening beats any slippage limit.
- OHLC open and close prints fake overnight edges, so use M1 execution prices.
- Peg or range mean reversion carries tail risk that stops cannot protect against (EURCHF, 2015).
- COT data and option pinning are weak or untestable with this data.
- Short-term trend following has degraded since about 2010.
- Small samples and fixed-spread backtests are red flags.
