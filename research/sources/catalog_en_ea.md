## Digest

**Recurring ideas.**
- Overfitting is the main reason EAs fail. On Quantopian, backtest Sharpe barely predicted live results across 888 algorithms, and the gap grew with the number of backtests run.
- Count trials. Tools: PBO/CSCV, deflated Sharpe, Harvey-Liu haircut, Reality Check/SPA, SPP grid median.
- Prefer multi-symbol pooling, few parameters and wide parameter plateaus.
- Keep separate build, validation and test eras, then incubate live for at least 6 months.

**Most promising new testable rules.**
1. Osler round-number cascades: stops cluster just beyond xx00/xx50. Trade the break 5-10 pips past the level. Fade the first touch, where take-profits sit.
2. Weekend-gap fade: if the Sunday open is more than X pips from Friday's close, fade toward that close with a Monday time stop. Skip gaps after news.
3. Random entry + ATR trailing stop (Basso) as the null for every rule. Run entry × exit factorial tests (LeBeau).
4. Turnaround Tuesday on the indices.
5. A portfolio whose members' losing-day correlation stays at or below 0.3, with a Monte Carlo pass probability under prop rules (5% daily / 10% total).

**Robustness battery to add.**
- Noise test (±⅓ ATR) and shifted-bar test using M1.
- Spread ×2, +1-bar delay, drop the top 5% of trades, ±20% parameters.
- Random-data bias ceiling (Fernandez).
- Cost sweep at 0, 1, 2 and 4 bp: intraday FX rules died at realistic costs (Dempster; Neely-Weller).

**Warnings.**
- Rollover spread spikes around 17:00 NY.
- 20+ pip NFP slippage.
- "99% modelling quality" only measures tick density.
- Demo feeds are not live feeds.
- Martingale and grid systems blow up on one-way trends.
- Vendor and Myfxbook records are survivorship-selected.
- Low-liquidity summers kill short live runs.
- MA and filter FX edges had decayed by the early 1990s.
